Using AI safely in the shop

Last updated September 3, 2026. Practical guidance, not legal advice, and deliberately blunt. Questions: support@bainbridgeai.ai.

The short version

An AI assistant is a place your words go. The sources on this site never see your conversation, only the search your assistant sends. The assistant itself sees everything you type, and what happens to that depends on your account and one or two settings. Two habits cover most of the risk: describe the job, not the customer, and check the training setting once instead of assuming.

The habit that does most of the work

Describe the job, not the customer. The vehicle, the operations, the hours, the exact sentence the adjuster wrote: that is everything these lookups need. Not one question on the front page of this site needs a customer's name to answer. "2022 Silverado, quarter replacement, they short-paid feather, prime, and block" gets you the same citations as the full claim file would, and it puts nobody's identity into anybody's system.

What a free account does with your words

On the free personal tiers of the big assistants, your conversations can be used to train future models, and portions of some conversations can be read by people doing that work. That is not a scandal; it is in each product's terms, and it is part of why the free tier is free. But it means a free chat is the wrong place for anything you would not put in an email to a stranger.

Our recommendation, plainly: if work conversations ever touch a real customer or a real claim, use a paid plan and turn the training setting off. Then open the setting and confirm it, because defaults change and vary by account. As of this writing:

  • Claude: Settings, then Privacy. The choice about using your chats to improve the model lives there.
  • ChatGPT: Settings, then Data controls. The switch is named along the lines of "Improve the model for everyone."
  • Gemini: it runs through Google's activity system, called Keep Activity or Gemini Apps Activity depending on where you look. One honest wrinkle: Gemini requires Keep Activity to be on before it will talk to custom connections like these sources. That is Google's rule, not ours, and it is a real trade to make knowingly. Claude and ChatGPT have no such requirement.

Setting names move around. If the words above are not there, search the settings for "improve" or "activity" and read what you find.

Never paste these on a free personal account

  • Customer names, phone numbers, or addresses attached to a vehicle or a claim.
  • Whole claim files, or estimate printouts with the customer block still on them. Crop or retype the part you actually need.
  • Card or bank details. No plan makes that a good idea.
  • Employee matters: pay, discipline, medical anything.
  • Photos that catch faces, license plates, or paperwork on the bench. Damage photos are fine; look at the background first.

A VIN by itself identifies a vehicle, not a person, and a recall check by VIN is routine. What to avoid is stacking a VIN together with the customer's identity in the same conversation on a free account.

Assistants are confidently wrong sometimes

Not wrong in an obvious way; wrong in a fluent, plausible way, with a straight face. That is the nature of the tool, and no setting turns it off. It is the entire reason RepairMCP answers carry an inquiry number, a campaign number, or a section number with a date: the citation is your check. Before a number goes into an estimate note, a supplement, or a letter, open the original and read it. It takes under a minute, and it is the difference between "the AI said so" and documentation.

One more behavior worth knowing: an assistant reads whatever text is in front of it, and text can steer it. Connections that can write, send mail, or move files deserve far more caution than connections that only look things up. Everything on this site is the second kind: every operation is a read, there are no accounts, and there is nothing to write with.

Where RepairMCP fits in this

The privacy risk in an AI workflow sits in the assistant, because the assistant is what holds your conversation. The sources here were built to add nothing to that risk:

  • They receive search terms, nothing else. Never the conversation, never your files, never your customer data.
  • They have no accounts and no sign-in, so there is nothing of yours for them to hold.
  • They only read. Claude files their tools under Read-only and ChatGPT tags every action READ, and both labels come from those products, not from us.
  • This site sets no cookies, runs no scripts, and loads nothing from anyone else.

The full terms and privacy statement, including how VINs are handled on recall checks, is at Terms and privacy.

Use it like a professional tool

Everything above condenses to this: these assistants are provided by their makers under their makers' terms, and using them is your call to make with open eyes, the same as any vendor decision in the shop. Free tiers are genuinely useful for questions that touch nobody's identity. The moment real customers and real claims enter the chat, pay for the plan, turn training off, and keep the habit of describing the job rather than the person. Print this page, hand it to whoever writes your estimates, and you have a policy.
