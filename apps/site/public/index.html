Set it up in Claude

About two minutes, once. After that it is there in every conversation. The screenshots below show the DEG connector; every source connects exactly the same way. The URL you paste in step three is the only difference, so to add NHTSA or Washington, run the same five steps with their URL from the top of the page.

  1. Open Settings

    Click your initials at the bottom left corner, then Settings.

  2. Go to Connectors and add a custom one

    In the settings window, Connectors is in the left list, under Customize. Open it, click Add, then Add custom connector.

  3. Name it and paste the URL

    Give it a name you will recognize later. The screenshots here use DEG Inquiry Search; for the other sources, names like NHTSA or Washington Law work well. Then paste the URL of the source you are adding. For DEG that is:

    https://deg.repairmcp.com/mcp

    Open Advanced settings if you want to see them, then leave them alone. There is no client ID and no secret to fill in. Click Add.

  4. Click Connect

    Adding it and turning it on are two different things, and this is the step people miss. Claude will tell you it is not connected yet. Click Connect.

  5. See what it is allowed to do

    It now shows as connected, with its six tools listed under a heading that reads Read-only tools. That heading is Claude's own label for what the connector offers, not ours.

    One more thing to expect: the first time it actually searches, Claude asks permission right in the chat. Choose Always allow and it will not ask again. Now go ask it about a job you argued over last week.

Which Claude plans this works on

Custom connectors work on Claude Free, Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise. On the Free plan you can have one custom connector at a time, so pick the source you argue from most; on paid plans the three sit side by side. On Team and Enterprise an owner adds them in the organization settings first, then everyone else turns them on from their own connector list.

Set it up in ChatGPT

Longer than Claude, because ChatGPT keeps custom connectors behind a developer setting. It is worth doing once. Web browser only. As with Claude, the screenshots show the DEG connector, and every other source connects the same way: same steps, its URL from the top of the page.

Which ChatGPT accounts this works on

OpenAI's own documentation says custom connectors need a paid account. In practice, every screenshot below was taken on a free ChatGPT account in August 2026: the developer setting was there, the connector was created, and it worked.

So take the paid-plan requirement as what OpenAI publishes rather than what you will definitely hit. This may differ by account and it may change without notice. If you cannot find the developer setting, that is the documented behaviour and a paid plan is the answer. It is worth looking before you assume you need one. Either way this has to be done in a web browser, not the phone app.

  1. Turn on developer mode

    Open ChatGPT in a browser. Go to Settings, then Security and login, and scroll to Developer mode. Switch it on.

    It is flagged Elevated risk and described as letting you add unverified connectors that could modify or erase data. That is an accurate description of the category. The section further down deals with it properly.

  2. Open the Plugins settings

    Still in Settings, open Plugins. With developer mode on, a Developer mode entry now appears here too.

  3. Start a new one

    Go to your plugins page and click the plus button beside the search box, at the top right.

  4. Change Authentication to No Auth

    A form opens. Before anything else, look at Authentication. It starts on OAuth, which is wrong for this one. These connectors have no sign-in, so there is no key to paste and nothing to approve. Change it to No Auth.

    Get this wrong and it will fail to connect for a reason the error message will not make obvious.

  5. Fill it in, then read the warning

    Name it for the source you are adding. The screenshots use DEG. The description is optional. Leave Connection on Server URL and paste that source's URL. For DEG:

    https://deg.repairmcp.com/mcp

    Then the warning appears, with a box you have to tick before Create will do anything. Do not click past it. The next section quotes it in full and answers it.

  6. Connect it

    ChatGPT shows a card asking whether to add it. Click Connect.

  7. See what it is allowed to do

    Open it and you get the list of what it can actually do. Every one is tagged READ. Again, that is ChatGPT's label for the connector, not ours. Now go ask it about a job.

About that warning box

Word for word, here is what it says:

Custom MCP servers introduce risk.

I understand and want to continue. OpenAI hasn't reviewed this MCP server. Attackers may attempt to steal your data or trick the model into taking unintended actions, including destroying data.

All of that is fair, and none of it is boilerplate. A connector is outside code that ChatGPT is allowed to call. Some connectors can write: send mail, change records, move money. Some can be talked into doing something you never asked for by text they read along the way. OpenAI cannot tell which kind you are adding, so they warn about every one of them the same way.

Why these sit at the low end of that range.

  • They only read, and you do not have to take that on faith. Claude files every tool under a heading that reads Read-only, and ChatGPT tags every action READ. Both screenshots above show it.
  • They hold public data: inquiries already published free at degweb.org, NHTSA's public recall and complaint services, and law text published by the government.
  • There is no sign-in and no token, which is why you set Authentication to No Auth. They have no route to your account, your files, your estimates, or your management system, because they were never handed one.
  • What each one can do is fixed and published at its URL, and every operation is a lookup.

You still have to tick the box. That is by design. OpenAI is asking you to confirm you trust the source. Read the four points above, decide for yourself, tick it, and carry on.

What to ask it

Once a source is connected, talk to it the way you would talk to another estimator. Plain shop language works, with one habit worth building: name the source in the ask, the DEG, NHTSA, Washington, or Montana. Without the name, the assistant may answer from its own memory or the open web instead of searching the real records. You can tell which one you got: a real search comes back with inquiry numbers, campaign numbers, or section numbers with dates, not web links. Every prompt below returns real, citable records today. Steal them.

Fighting a denial or a short-pay

  • "Search the DEG for inquiries supporting weld-thru primer as a not-included operation on a quarter panel replacement."
  • "I'm getting short-paid on feather, prime, and block after a panel replacement. What DEG inquiries support paying it?"

Writing a supplement

  • "Find DEG inquiries supporting setup and measure as its own line on a frame bench."
  • "Search the DEG: what have the estimating systems said about structural adhesive and anti-flutter foam on a roof replacement?"

Blueprinting a repair

  • "Check the DEG: is aiming the headlamps included after a bumper replacement, or a separate operation?"
  • "Find DEG inquiries about cavity wax and corrosion protection after sectioning."
  • "Search the DEG: is a seat belt inspection required after a collision, and is it a paid operation?"

Checking recalls, complaints, and federal law

  • "Check NHTSA for open recalls on a 2020 Ford Transit before we deliver it."
  • "Search NHTSA complaints for steering issues on a 2020 Ford Transit. How many are there, and were any crashes?"
  • "What does federal law say about a body shop disabling a safety device? Ask NHTSA for the actual statute section."

Quoting Washington law

  • "Ask Washington law: is the insurer allowed to deny storage charges?"
  • "Search Washington law: can the insurer steer my customer away from our shop?"
  • "Ask Washington law: what meal and rest breaks do my painters get?"

Quoting Montana law

  • "Ask Montana law: can the insurer make me use their body shop?"
  • "Search Montana law: the adjuster deleted operations that are in the estimating system we both agreed to use."
  • "Ask Montana law: what does the Wrongful Discharge Act say about probation?"

If ChatGPT answers with web links instead

That means it searched the open web and never used the connector. The usual cause is simpler than it looks: the connector is not actually installed on the account you are asking from. It is easy to set it up on one account and ask from another. Open Settings, then Plugins, and confirm the source is there and connected; if it is not, the steps above take about two minutes. Then name it, the way every prompt above does. When the connector answers, you get a Called tool note and numbers with dates; web links mean it did not.

Then put the number in writing

Every answer carries its citation: an inquiry number, a recall campaign number, or a law section number, with the date. That citation is the point: paste it into your estimate notes or your supplement so the reviewer can open the same page you did. The answer in the screenshot at the top of this page is one of these, a welded side panel extension where the published time moved from 1.0 hours to 3.5 after the shop documented the factory spot welds.

And when the answer is not in there, it says so rather than guessing. Every reply states how current its data is.

Where the data comes from

The inquiries come from the Database Enhancement Gateway at degweb.org, which publishes every one of them free to anyone. This is a copy that an AI assistant can search quickly, kept current and cited back to the original. Every answer carries the inquiry number and the date so you can open the real page and read it yourself.

The mix follows the source: most inquiries are CCC, with smaller sets for Mitchell and Audatex. Nothing is filtered out or rewritten. Dates, spellings, and vehicle names are left exactly as the source has them, typos included, because a citation that quietly cleans things up is not a citation.

NHTSA answers come straight from the agency's public services at the moment you ask, so recall and complaint data is as current as NHTSA itself, and the federal safety statute is captured from the United States Code with its own currency stated. Washington law is captured word for word from the state legislature's site at leg.wa.gov, and every section carries the date its current text took effect, straight from the rule's own history note.

How it behaves

  • It only reads. There is no operation that writes anything, anywhere.
  • No account, no sign-in, no cost. Paste the URL and it works.
  • It does not see your data. No estimates, no files, no customer information, no management system.
  • Nothing is stored about you. This site sets no cookies, runs no scripts, and carries no analytics of any kind.
  • The source is open. Apache 2.0, and the whole thing is on GitHub if you want to read it.
