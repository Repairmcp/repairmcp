Set it up in Claude

About two minutes, once. After that it is there in every conversation. Most of the screenshots below show the DEG connector; every source connects exactly the same way. The URL you paste in step three is the only difference, so to add NHTSA or a state, run the same six steps with their URL from the top of the page.

  1. Open Settings

    Click your initials at the bottom left corner, then Settings.

  2. Go to Connectors and add a custom one

    In the settings window, Connectors is in the left list, under Customize. Open it, click Add, then Add custom connector.

  3. Name it and paste the URL

    Give it a name you will recognize later. The screenshots here use DEG Inquiry Search; for the other sources, names like NHTSA or Washington Law work well. Then paste the URL of the source you are adding. For DEG that is:

    https://deg.repairmcp.com/mcp

    Open Advanced settings if you want to see them, then leave them alone. There is no client ID and no secret to fill in. Click Add.

  4. Click Connect

    Adding it and turning it on are two different things, and this is the step people miss. Claude will tell you it is not connected yet. Click Connect.

  5. See what it is allowed to do

    It now shows as connected, with its six tools listed under a heading that reads Read-only tools. That heading is Claude's own label for what the connector offers, not ours.

    One more thing to expect: the first time it actually searches, Claude asks permission right in the chat. Choose Always allow there and it will not ask again. Or settle it up front in settings, which is the last step.

  6. Set the whole list to Always allow

    Open the connector from your Connectors list and find Tool permissions. The dropdown next to Read-only tools sets every tool at once. Set it to Always allow and Claude uses the source mid-answer instead of stopping to ask. Every tool on every source here only reads, so the most any of them can ever do is look something up.

    This shot shows the Montana connector; the page looks the same for each source. Now go ask it about a job you are documenting this week.

Which Claude plans this works on

Custom connectors work on Claude Free, Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise. On the Free plan you can have one custom connector at a time, so pick the source you document from most; on paid plans the three sit side by side. On Team and Enterprise an owner adds them in the organization settings first, then everyone else turns them on from their own connector list.

Set it up in ChatGPT

Longer than Claude, because ChatGPT keeps custom connectors behind a developer setting. It is worth doing once. Web browser only. As with Claude, the screenshots show the DEG connector, and every other source connects the same way: same steps, its URL from the top of the page.

Which ChatGPT accounts this works on

OpenAI's own documentation says custom connectors need a paid account. In practice, every screenshot below was taken on a free ChatGPT account in August 2026: the developer setting was there, the connector was created, and it worked.

So take the paid-plan requirement as what OpenAI publishes rather than what you will definitely hit. This may differ by account and it may change without notice. If you cannot find the developer setting, that is the documented behaviour and a paid plan is the answer. It is worth looking before you assume you need one. Either way this has to be done in a web browser, not the phone app.

  1. Turn on developer mode

    Open ChatGPT in a browser. Go to Settings, then Security and login, and scroll to Developer mode. Switch it on.

    It is flagged Elevated risk and described as letting you add unverified connectors that could modify or erase data. That is an accurate description of the category. The section further down deals with it properly.

  2. Open the Plugins settings

    Still in Settings, open Plugins. With developer mode on, a Developer mode entry now appears here too.

  3. Start a new one

    Go to your plugins page and click the plus button beside the search box, at the top right.

  4. Change Authentication to No Auth

    A form opens. Before anything else, look at Authentication. It starts on OAuth, which is wrong for this one. These connectors have no sign-in, so there is no key to paste and nothing to approve. Change it to No Auth.

    Get this wrong and it will fail to connect for a reason the error message will not make obvious.

  5. Fill it in, then read the warning

    Name it for the source you are adding. The screenshots use DEG. The description is optional. Leave Connection on Server URL and paste that source's URL. For DEG:

    https://deg.repairmcp.com/mcp

    Then the warning appears, with a box you have to tick before Create will do anything. Do not click past it. The next section quotes it in full and answers it.

  6. Connect it

    ChatGPT shows a card asking whether to add it. Click Connect.

  7. See what it is allowed to do

    Open it and you get the list of what it can actually do. Every one is tagged READ. Again, that is ChatGPT's label for the connector, not ours. Now go ask it about a job.

About that warning box

Word for word, here is what it says:

Custom MCP servers introduce risk.

I understand and want to continue. OpenAI hasn't reviewed this MCP server. Attackers may attempt to steal your data or trick the model into taking unintended actions, including destroying data.

All of that is fair, and none of it is boilerplate. A connector is outside code that ChatGPT is allowed to call. Some connectors can write: send mail, change records, move money. Some can be talked into doing something you never asked for by text they read along the way. OpenAI cannot tell which kind you are adding, so they warn about every one of them the same way.

Why these sit at the low end of that range.

  • They only read, and you do not have to take that on faith. Claude files every tool under a heading that reads Read-only, ChatGPT tags every action READ, and Gemini lists the six actions it synced before you connect, every one a lookup. The screenshots in each section show it.
  • They hold public data: inquiries already published free at degweb.org, NHTSA's public recall and complaint services, and law text published by the government.
  • There is no sign-in and no token, which is why you set Authentication to No Auth. They have no route to your account, your files, your estimates, or your management system, because they were never handed one.
  • What each one can do is fixed and published at its URL, and every operation is a lookup.

You still have to tick the box. That is by design. OpenAI is asking you to confirm you trust the source. Read the four points above, decide for yourself, tick it, and carry on.

Set it up in Gemini

The newest of the three. Google added custom connections to Gemini at the end of June 2026, and they live inside Spark, Gemini's task-running mode, not in plain Gemini chat. Like the other two sections, everything below was walked on a real account in September 2026, and the screenshots are what actually happened. The walk-through connects DEG; every other source connects the same way with its URL from the top of the page. Adding a custom app only works in a web browser; once it is added, it works in the phone app too.

Which Google accounts this works on

Google gates this more tightly than Anthropic or OpenAI do. You need all of the following:

  • A paid Google AI Pro or Google AI Ultra plan. The free plan does not have it. The screenshots below were taken on Pro.
  • A personal Google account, the kind that ends in @gmail.com. A company address like bob@bobscollisionshop.com is a Workspace account, and Google excludes those, along with school accounts. This one bites quietly: signed in with a work account, the custom apps area simply is not on the page. No error, just absent. Check which account you are signed in with before anything else.
  • A United States account, held by someone 18 or over, per Google's help page as of September 2026. Google has been widening where Spark is available, so if you are outside the US it is worth checking whether it has reached you.
  • The Keep Activity setting turned on. Spark cannot reach any connected app with it off. Keep Activity is also the Google setting that governs what happens to your conversations, so it is a real trade, not a checkbox. It is covered honestly on Using AI safely in the shop.

  1. Turn on Spark

    Go to gemini.google.com in a browser. In the left sidebar, right under the Gemini logo, there is a small switch. That is the Spark toggle, and everything below lives behind it. Flip it and the page changes to Put Gemini Spark to work for you, with a BETA tag in the corner. If you have never heard of Spark, you are not behind: it is Gemini's mode for running tasks, it is new, and this is a fine first reason to use it.

  2. Open the Apps page and add the link

    Open Settings & help at the bottom left, then Connected apps. Google's own partner apps sit at the top; scroll to Custom apps for Spark, paste the URL of the source you are adding into the Add a custom app link field, and click Next. For DEG that is:

    https://deg.repairmcp.com/mcp

    The dialog has an Advanced Settings area with two sign-in fields. These sources have no sign-in, so both stay empty. If the custom apps area is not on the page at all, you are signed in with a work account or do not have Spark yet; see the account list above.

  3. Read the warning, then tick the box

    Google shows a warning before it will connect, and just like ChatGPT's, it is fair and worth reading. The heart of it, word for word:

    Google does not control, monitor, or secure this MCP server (shown above). Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers may request data beyond what is needed to complete your request, including data you may consider sensitive. Supervise the MCP server. Make sure you understand the actions it supports and trust the provider before proceeding.

    I understand and accept the security and privacy risks of connecting this custom app.

    The reasons these sources sit at the safe end of that warning are the same four listed in the ChatGPT section above: they only read, they hold public data, they have no sign-in to steal, and every operation is a lookup. Decide for yourself, tick the box, and click Connect.

  4. See what it is allowed to do, and note the name

    Gemini syncs the source and shows you exactly what it found: six actions, every one a lookup. This screen also names the app, and the name matters more here than in Claude or ChatGPT, because you will call it by that name in your prompts. DEG arrives as RepairMCP DEG. You can edit it here; whatever it says when you click Connect is what you say in step six. One note: the screenshots in this section were taken just before we tidied that name up, so they show the older Repairmcp Deg.

  5. Confirm it is on

    Back on the Apps page, the source now has its own card under Custom apps for Spark, with its toggle on and a last-sync time. To add another source, click Add a custom app next to it and run the same steps with the next URL.

  6. Guide it by name

    Gemini calls these apps, not connectors, and it will not guess. We asked it to "use the DEG connector" and it went to Google Search instead, the same way ChatGPT reaches for the web. The ask that works names the app exactly the way the Apps page does:

    "Use the App RepairMCP DEG, and find DEG inquiries supporting setup and measure as its own line on a frame bench. The estimate is in CCC."

    Typing @ in the prompt box also brings up your connected apps to pick from. Either way, watch its reasoning: the app's icon appears while it searches. That is how you know it went to the source and not the web.

  7. Get an answer you can put in writing

    What came back, on the first try: the MOTOR Guide to Estimating position that frame machine setup and measure times are not developed by MOTOR and not included in any other operation, with the resolved inquiries behind it cited by number and date, starting with DEG #21350. The same citation discipline as the other two assistants, from the same corpus.

What to ask it

Once a source is connected, talk to it the way you would talk to another estimator. Plain shop language works, with one habit worth building: name the source AND the estimating database in the ask. The source is the DEG, NHTSA, Washington, Montana, Colorado, Texas, or California; without it, the assistant may answer from its own memory or the open web instead of searching the real records. The estimating database, CCC, Mitchell, or Audatex, matters on every DEG question, because each system publishes its own labor times and included operations, and an inquiry resolved against one does not settle the other two. In ChatGPT, go one step further: it will happily reach for its own web search instead, so call the connector by name and tell it to use it. The note at the bottom of this section shows the wording. Gemini is the same, with its own vocabulary: open with "Use the App" plus the name on your Apps page, like "Use the App RepairMCP DEG". You can tell which answer you got: a real search comes back with inquiry numbers, campaign numbers, or section numbers with dates, not web links. Every prompt below returns real, citable records today. Steal them.

Fighting a denial or a short-pay

  • "Search the DEG for CCC inquiries supporting weld-thru primer as a not-included operation on a quarter panel replacement."
  • "I write in Mitchell and I'm getting short-paid on feather, prime, and block after a panel replacement. What DEG inquiries support paying it?"

Writing a supplement

  • "Find DEG inquiries supporting setup and measure as its own line on a frame bench. The estimate is in CCC."
  • "Search the DEG: what has CCC said about structural adhesive and anti-flutter foam on a roof replacement?"

Blueprinting a repair

  • "Check the DEG: in CCC, is aiming the headlamps included after a bumper replacement, or a separate operation?"
  • "Find DEG inquiries about cavity wax and corrosion protection after sectioning on a CCC estimate."
  • "Search the DEG: is a seat belt inspection required after a collision, and does Audatex pay it as its own operation?"

Checking recalls, complaints, and federal law

  • "Check NHTSA for open recalls on a 2020 Ford Transit before we deliver it."
  • "Search NHTSA complaints for steering issues on a 2020 Ford Transit. How many are there, and were any crashes?"
  • "What does federal law say about a body shop disabling a safety device? Ask NHTSA for the actual statute section."

Quoting Washington law

  • "Ask Washington law: is the insurer allowed to deny storage charges?"
  • "Search Washington law: can the insurer steer my customer away from our shop?"
  • "Ask Washington law: what meal and rest breaks do my painters get?"

Quoting Montana law

  • "Ask Montana law: can the insurer make me use their body shop?"
  • "Search Montana law: the adjuster deleted operations that are in the estimating system we both agreed to use."
  • "Ask Montana law: what does the Wrongful Discharge Act say about probation?"

Quoting Colorado law

  • "Ask Colorado law: can the insurer make my customer use their network shop?"
  • "Ask Colorado law: the adjuster went silent on our supplement, what deadline applies?"

Quoting Texas law

  • "Ask Texas law: can the insurer make my customer use their network shop?"
  • "Ask Texas law: the customer wants to invoke appraisal on a lowball estimate."
  • "Ask Texas law: what does the insurer owe if they blow the claim payment deadlines?"
  • "Ask Texas law: the customer never paid for the repair, can I hold the car?"

Quoting California law

  • "Ask California law: the insurer caps paint and materials at a flat amount, what does the law say?"
  • "Ask California law: the adjuster says the OEM procedure is not necessary and will not pay for it."
  • "Ask California law: the adjuster says the prevailing rate is whatever their labor rate survey shows."
  • "Ask California law: do I have to pay my flat-rate techs separately for rest breaks?"

If ChatGPT answers with web links instead

That means it searched the open web and never used the connector. There are two usual causes, and both are quick fixes.

The connector is not on the account you are asking from. It is easy to set it up on one account and ask from another. Open Settings, then Plugins, and confirm the source is there and connected; if it is not, the steps above take about two minutes.

The connector is there and ChatGPT reached for web search anyway. ChatGPT favors its own web search, and a polite ask can lose to it. Be direct: call the connector by the exact name you gave it when you added it, and tell ChatGPT to use it. "Use the Colorado Law connector: the adjuster went silent on our supplement, what deadline applies?" If it starts listing websites anyway, interrupt and say "use the connector." We have watched it drop the web results and switch mid-answer on exactly that.

When the connector answers, you get a Called tool note and numbers with dates; web links mean it did not.

Then put the number in writing

Every answer carries its citation: an inquiry number, a recall campaign number, or a law section number, with the date. That citation is the point: paste it into your estimate notes or your supplement so the reviewer can open the same page you did. The answer in the screenshot at the top of this page is one of these, a welded side panel extension where the published time moved from 1.0 hours to 3.5 after the shop documented the factory spot welds.

And when the answer is not in there, it says so rather than guessing. Every reply states how current its data is.

Where the data comes from

The inquiries come from the Database Enhancement Gateway at degweb.org, which publishes every one of them free to anyone. This is a copy that an AI assistant can search quickly, kept current and cited back to the original. Every answer carries the inquiry number and the date so you can open the real page and read it yourself.

The mix follows the source: most inquiries are CCC, with smaller sets for Mitchell and Audatex. Nothing is filtered out or rewritten. Dates, spellings, and vehicle names are left exactly as the source has them, typos included, because a citation that quietly cleans things up is not a citation.

NHTSA answers come straight from the agency's public services at the moment you ask, so recall and complaint data is as current as NHTSA itself, and the federal safety statute is captured from the United States Code with its own currency stated. Washington, Montana, Colorado, and Texas law are each captured word for word from that state's own official publications, and every section carries the date its current text took effect, straight from the source's own history note. California statutes and Cal/OSHA orders come from the state's own sites the same way; its other regulations come from the Legal Information Institute's mirror of the California Code of Regulations, with each section's own amendment history kept.

How it behaves

  • It only reads. There is no operation that writes anything, anywhere.
  • No account, no sign-in, no cost. Paste the URL and it works.
  • It does not see your data. No estimates, no files, no customer information, no management system.
  • Nothing is stored about you. This site sets no cookies, runs no scripts, and carries no analytics of any kind.
  • The source is open. Apache 2.0, and the whole thing is on GitHub if you want to read it.

The other half of privacy is the assistant itself: what you type into Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini is governed by your account with them, not by RepairMCP. There is a plain-language page here about exactly that, including what a free account does with your conversations, the training settings worth checking, and what never to paste: Using AI safely in the shop.

Support the source
